- Almost all algorithms doing hierarchical clustering are bottom-up
  
- ## Types of hierarchical clustering
	- Bottom-up / agglomerative
		- Merges most similar / nearest pair of clusters
		- Stops when all points are merged at the center
	- top-down / divisive
		- starts w/ all data points in 1 cluster, the root, splits down
	- Bottom-up is more popular
	- Key is measure of distance, how do measure distance of clusters?
		- Most popular is furthest distance
		  
- ## Measuring distance of 2 clusters
	- Results in variations of the algorithm
		- ### Single link
			- distance b/w 2 clusters is the distance b/w the two closest data points in the clusters, one from each
			- May cause the [[chain effect]] by noisy points
		- ### Complete Link
			- The distance b/w two clusters is the distance of 2 furthest data points in the two clusters
			- Sensitive to outliers due to distance
		- ### Average Link
	- ## Complexity
		- All algorithms are at least $O(n^2)$ because we have to try every data point combination
		- Hard to use for large data sets
			- We can use sampling, scale-up methods

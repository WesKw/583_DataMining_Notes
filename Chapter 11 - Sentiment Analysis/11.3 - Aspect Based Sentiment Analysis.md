- ## Aspect Sentiment Classification
	- for each aspect, identify the sentiment about it
	- Work based on sentences
		- however sentences may have multiple aspects with different opinions
		- E.g.: Battery life and picture quality are great (+), but the view founder is small (-)
	- Almost all approaches make use of opinion words and phrases
	- It's difficult in the sense that it's easy to get 70% for simple sentences, but there are a lot of difficult cases
		- Some opinion words have context dependent orientations
	- Lexicon-based approach: Opinion words/phrases
		- Parsing: simple sentences
- ## Sentiment Shifters
	- also called **valence shifters**
	- words and phrases that can shift opinion orientations
	- Negation words (not, never, cannot)
	- model auxiliary verbs (would, should, could, etc)
	- Very complicated
	- Some presuppositional items can change opinions too (barely, hardly)
	- fail, omit, neglect
	- Sarcasm changes orientation too
	- Basic rules of opinions:
		- Opinions/sentiments are governed by many rules
		- Opinion words or phrase: "I love this car"
		- Desirable or undesirable facts: "After my wife and I slept on it for 2 weeks, I noticed a mountain in the middle of the mattress "
			- P ::= positive opinion word / phrase / desirable fact
			- N ::= negative opinion word / phrase / undesirable fact
		- High low, increased, decreased quantity of a positive potential item (PPI) or negative potential item (NPI)
		- Decreased and increased quantity of an opinionated item: "This drug reduced my pain significantly"
		- Deviation from the desired value range: "This drug increased my blood pressure to 200"
		- Producing and consuming resources and wastes
		- How do we deal with this? We usually need some kind of parser
	- Some interesting sentences
		- "Trying out chrome because firefox keeps crashing"
			- firefox - negative; no opinion about chrome
			- We need to segment the sentence into clauses to decide that crashing only applies to firefox(?)
		- "I changed to Audi because BMW is so expensive"
			- This is essentially a comparison
		- "I did not buy BMW because of the high price"
		- "I am so happy that my iPhone is nothing like my old ugly Droid"
		- 2 sentences from paint reviews:
			- "One coat of paintX covers the wood color."
			- "Three coats of paintY cover the wood color"
			- PaintX is clearly better than paintY, but the system doesn't know if one is better than the other.
		- "My goal is to get a tv with good picture quality."
		- "The top of the picture was brighter than the bottom"
		- Conditional sentences are hard to deal with
			- "If I can find a good camera, I will buy it."
			- However, they can also have opinions
				- "If you are looking for a good phone, buy Nokia"
		- Questions are also hard to handle.
			- "Are there great perks for employees?"
			- "Any idea how to fix this lousy Sony camera?"
		- Sarcastic sentences
			- "What a great car, it stopped working in the second day."
			- Happens *a lot* in political posts
		- - “This past Saturday, I bought a Nokia phone and my girlfriend bought a Motorola phone with Bluetooth. We called each other when we got home. The voice on my phone was not so clear, worse than my previous Samsung phone. The battery life was short too. My girlfriend was quite happy with her phone. I wanted a phone with good sound quality. So my purchase was a real disappointment. I returned the phone yesterday.”
			- Before language models, this was not do-able!
			- The system does not know which phone is the girlfriend's phone!
	- Aspect based opinion summarization:
		- must be quantitative
			- When we talk about opinions, we must be able to formally define what the problem is
			- We must define "what do we mean by the opinion"
			- "the opinion needs to be quantitative"
				- E.g. we need to be able to determine "70% of people like X, or 60% of people dislike Y"
- ## Emotion and its impact on AI
	- Emotion - complex, short-lived, intense psycho-physiological response to an internal or external event
	- **event-triggered**, high intensity, discrete
	- Prepares someone for an adaptive action
	- No agreed set of basic emotions
	- Emotion and opinions are not equivalent
	- Opinion: rational (+/-) view on something/target
		- Cannot say "I like"
	- Emotion: focusing on inner feeling
		- Does not have to have a target in the sentence
			- This makes emotion analysis more difficult than opinion analysis.
		- "I am angry", "There is sadness in her eyes"
		- 
	- Emotion is a quintuple
		- (entity, aspect, emotion_type, feeler, time)
		- Definition can also include the cause
		- "I am so mad with the hotel manager because he refused to refund my booking fee"
			- Entity: hotel
			- aspect: manager
			- emotion_type: anger
			- feeler: I
			- time: unknown
	- Emotion expressions
		- 1) use emotion or mood words / phrases
		- 2) describe emotion-related behaviors
		- 3) Use intensifiers
			- Common: very, so, extremely, awfully, etc
		- 4) Use superlatives
			- Can also express emotions
			- "This car is simply the best"
		- 5) Use pejorative (e.g. "He is a fascist"), laudatory ("He is a saint"), or sarcastic expressions
		- 6) Swearing, insulting, blaming, accusing, threatening expressions
	- Mood
		- A diffuse, low-intensity, and long lasting affective state w/ no specific object or clear cause
		- Objectless, low arousal, slow-changing
		- Biases information processing and creates a general action readiness
	- Personality
		- Enduring, organized patterns of thought, feeling, and behavior
		- Distinguish individuals
		- Highly stable traits, dispositional (tendencies), multidimensional
		- Shapes how individuals characteristically appraise events, experience emotions, regulate responses, and interact
	- hierarchy
		- *Personality* is the baseline
		- Ongoing *mood* can bias the appraisal of a neutral event
		- *emotional* reaction can be expressed in a way filtered by personality
	- Fast (Event): Emotion -> realtime appraisal tags stimuli w/ valence, arousal, action tendencies
	- Medium (Context): Mood -> Slowly changing baseline that biases appraisals
	- Slow (Disposition): Personality -> Traits parameterize the emotional system

	- Self-motivation for learning

		- AI models only do optimizations
		- In humans, if we only do optimization, we cannot be autonomous, humans decide what is and is not important
		- We are able to self-regulate

		- Long-term credit assignment: Humans operate on long time horizons
		- Social and abstract reinforcement: Social belonging, admiration, guilt, fairness - learning extends past primary needs (food, safety)
